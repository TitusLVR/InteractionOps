# UV Stripe

Unwraps each selected strip of faces into UV with as little stretch as it can: the strip is torn only where a tear lowers the stretch, and every piece is laid out with what you ask to be straight (its long sides, its ends), the layout itself chosen by measuring the stretch. Starting it (a key, the menu, the pie, the search) opens its interactive mode with the options UV Stripe was last used with: switch every option with a key and watch the result in the UV editor while the viewport shows the four sides of every piece, straight or free, and the faces whose UVs are still stretched (see *Stretched faces*); a click on a side makes it straight or free. An island that is no strip (a T, a fork, a slit) is cut into strips that are laid out as one (see *Islands that are no strip*). No UV editor work and no seams are needed: the seams are set to match the pieces. Edit Mesh mode.

**Hotkey:** Not bound by default — assign a key in *Preferences › iOps › Keymaps*, or run it from the operator search or the Edit pie (UV group). There is one entry, *UV Stripe*: it always starts the interactive mode below. The former *UV Stripe (Interactive)* entry is folded into it (2026-11-24): a key or pie slot bound to it moves to *UV Stripe* on its own.

While <kbd>V</kbd> is on, a line at the top of the HUD says so (*Picking lines to straighten · Alt: single edge · V to stop*). The status bar shows one fixed line: *LMB face: select piece · Shift: add · LMB edge: cut / mend · Ctrl + LMB: one edge · Q / W: selected, else all · Enter: confirm · Esc: cancel* (while an island waits for help: *LMB boundary vertex: corner · LMB edge: cut the island · Ctrl + LMB: one edge · Enter: confirm · Esc: cancel*), and while picking lines a line of its own.

What a click would change is drawn before the click, at the theme's line width for that colour, in the colour of what the click makes: the loop (<kbd>LMB</kbd>) or the edge (<kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>LMB</kbd>; <kbd>Alt</kbd>+<kbd>LMB</kbd> while picking lines) a click would cut in the preview colour, a line to straighten in the error colour, a cut or line the click takes back in the locked colour. With it the outline of the piece the click changes is drawn thin in the active colour (the piece whose face is under the cursor, where an edge lies on a tear between two pieces; for an island that is no strip, the island), so with several strips selected you see which one a click acts on; away from the edges that is the piece a click would select. The selected pieces are outlined wide in the active colour the whole time. Over nothing a click would change (a line laid straight already, a tear made by Split at Seams, empty space) nothing is drawn and the click does nothing. The colours, with the default theme: cuts yellow (the cuts made by hand, ring breaks, island cuts and Auto's tears), straightened lines red, the selection (the selected pieces, and thin the piece a click acts on) green; free sides are plain lines and straight sides blue. The selection and the hover lines are drawn opaque even where the theme keeps the active and locked colours faint. The cuts and the straightened lines stay drawn the whole time the interactive mode runs.

The plain wheel zooms the view; the wheel with a modifier works in every state of the interactive mode, while picking lines too. **Balance** (<kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Wheel</kbd>) is always in the HUD, for example *0.50 · Least Stretch < > Even Texel*; when no piece is laid out balanced the row is dimmed and says *no balanced piece to change*. While the wheel turns, the layout follows at once from a quick solve (the HUD says *Refining...*), and the full solve replaces it once the wheel has been still for 0.15 s. <kbd>Enter</kbd> waits for it, so the confirmed UVs equal those of the redo panel and of a plain call.

Every start of *UV Stripe* (a key, the menu, the pie, the search, or `bpy.ops.iops.mesh_uv_stripe('INVOKE_DEFAULT')` from a script) is this interactive mode. The options confirmed here become the last-used options of the next start and of Inset and Edge Slide; the redo panel, and a script calling the operator directly (`bpy.ops.iops.mesh_uv_stripe(...)`), lay the strips out at once with the options given, the same result without the interactive mode.

## Selecting pieces
Every piece has four sides, found by its corners: two long sides (the rails) and two ends. The viewport draws them all the time, a straight side in the closest colour (blue with the default theme) and a free side in the plain line colour. A long side is drawn as it is laid out (Stretch Limit may have relaxed it); an end is drawn as asked: a free end is not forced upright, it keeps its real slant.
- **LMB on a face**, away from its edges, selects the piece holding it (on an island waiting for help, the island); it becomes the only selected piece. <kbd>Shift</kbd>+<kbd>LMB</kbd> adds a piece to the selection or removes it. LMB on the only selected piece again, or over nothing, clears the selection. Near an edge LMB cuts instead (see *Marking cuts*); with Shift it always selects. The selected pieces are outlined in the active colour, at the theme's Active line width; the HUD shows a *Selected* row with their count while there are any.
- **Straight Sides** (<kbd>Q</kbd>) and **Ends** (<kbd>W</kbd>) with pieces selected change those pieces alone: every press moves all of them to the value after the first selected piece's, and the HUD rows show that value and how many pieces are selected (*Longer Side · 2 selected*). With nothing selected they cycle the value for every piece and drop every value of that kind set for pieces alone (<kbd>Q</kbd> the long sides, <kbd>W</kbd> the ends), so the keys always change what you see.
- A piece's own value is relaxed by **Stretch Limit** like the value for every piece: it is the same setting, only for that piece. (A side clicked straight in an older version was never relaxed; such a side kept by an older run's redo still lays out as it did.)
- A piece keeps its own values while it holds a face it had when they were set: a cut through it leaves them on both parts. Other keys (<kbd>E</kbd>, <kbd>A</kbd> and the rest) keep them.
- With both long sides free the piece is unfolded at its real shape and **Align** applies; its ends then follow the faces.
- The pieces' own values last for the redo panel; a new run of UV Stripe starts from Straight Sides and Ends for every piece.

## Breaking a ring
A closed ring is normally cut open at one cross edge (an existing seam, else its sharpest corner) and torn where a tear lowers the stretch. To lay a ring out as **one straight strip** instead, hover a cross edge (near it, not with Shift): it lights up, and on a ring that has no break yet <kbd>LMB</kbd> breaks the ring there and lays it out again. Every further click on another cross edge of the ring cuts it there by hand (see *Marking cuts*), so the cuts add up.
- A broken ring is laid out as an open strip starting at the break: the seams on its other cross edges no longer cut it (Mark Seams clears them and marks the break), but it is still torn where a tear lowers the stretch, like any open strip (a ring that unrolls without stretch, such as a band round a cylinder, stays one piece). Cuts you mark by hand still tear it (see *Marking cuts*), and a tear can be skipped by clicking it.
- Clicking the break again removes it; the other cuts stay. To move a break, click it to remove it, then click the new edge; each ring keeps its own break. The breaks last for the redo panel, but a new run of UV Stripe starts without them.

### A closed band several faces wide
A closed band wider than one face (two boundary loops, such as a washer or a band round a sphere several rows tall) is cut open along one whole line of edges from one boundary loop to the other and laid out as one wide strip: the two loops are the long sides, the two sides of the cut its ends, and it goes through the same layout as any wide strip (Straight Sides, Ends, Balance, Stretch Limit). The line is, first match: the break you picked (it wins over a seam line across the band, which is then not cut, and Mark Seams clears it); the seams inside the band when they are exactly one such line (Split at Seams on); else the band's wall seam as UV Pipe picks it (the shortest line from loop to loop, a tie to the lowest position). Mark Seams marks the cut.
- <kbd>LMB</kbd> on any edge of a line across the band (the way a loop cut runs, straight on through every vertex with four edges) cuts it there; the hover shows the whole line. The first cut opens the band there; every further cut adds a cut, and the band is then cut into as many wide strips as it has cuts, each its own strip. <kbd>LMB</kbd> on the line of a cut removes that cut alone; with none left the band goes back to its default cut.
- A band much longer on one loop than on the other (a flat washer) is an arc when unrolled: as one straight strip its rows must stretch. Straight Sides Both then usually stretches beyond Stretch Limit, and the band is relaxed to its real shape (the arc); raise Stretch Limit to keep it straight.

## Marking cuts
<kbd>LMB</kbd> on a cross edge (near it, not with Shift) of a selected strip one face wide, open or closed, cuts there; the loop across the strip is that cross edge itself, so <kbd>LMB</kbd> and <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>LMB</kbd> do the same. Any number of cuts can be made, one click each. What a click does depends on the edge:
- On a closed ring with no break yet: the ring is broken there (see *Breaking a ring*).
- The ring's break: the break is removed.
- A cut you marked: the cut is removed.
- A tear made where it lowers the stretch: that tear is skipped, and the pieces on both sides join. The skipped tear stays drawn in the viewport, green with the default theme.
- A skipped tear: the tear comes back.
- A tear made by Split at Seams: nothing changes. Turn Split at Seams off, or clear the seam, to lay the strip across it.
- Any other cross edge: the strip is cut there by hand.

A cut marked by hand tears the strip whatever Split at Seams is set to, also on a broken ring, and Mark Seams marks it like any other tear. A skipped tear only stops the stretch-lowering tear there. The tears and the cuts made by hand (marked cuts, a ring's break, the cuts of a wide strip, a band or an island) are drawn in the preview colour, over the sides (a cut across a wide strip is the end of both its parts). The HUD shows how many cuts are marked by hand. Like the breaks, the marked cuts and skipped tears last for the redo panel, and a new run starts without them.

## Cutting a wide strip
On a strip several faces wide one cross edge alone does not tear it. <kbd>LMB</kbd> on an edge (near it, not with Shift) takes the loop through it the way UV Pipe traces its loops: from the edge it runs on both ways, at every vertex by the straightest edge, through quads, triangles and n-gons alike, until it reaches a long side, an end, a cut or tear already made, or a turn sharper than 120° between its edges (UV Pipe's default Loop Angle). So it runs across the strip from one long side to the other, or along it from end to end, and a second loop ends where it meets the first. The hover shows exactly the edges it cuts: the piece is cut along it into two parts, each its own strip (see *Islands that are no strip*). A loop is offered only when both its ends reach a side, an end or a cut, and the piece under the cursor, cut there, still reads as strips without any further automatic cut; where the traced loop is not offered, the line straight on through every vertex with four edges is (from one long side to the other), else nothing lights up and a click does nothing. Cuts add up: every click on another loop cuts there too. <kbd>LMB</kbd> on any edge of a cut mends that whole cut and leaves the others. <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>LMB</kbd> cuts the one edge under the cursor (along the line of edges through it, when there is one). The cuts last for the redo panel, and a new run starts without them.

### Auto tears a wide strip at its bends
A strip several faces wide that cannot be laid out straight within Stretch Limit (a rail Stretch Limit would relax, or a face stretching beyond it) is torn first where it bends within its surface: along the lines of edges across it from a rail vertex that turns by more than 15° (a mitre, say). Tearing comes before stretching, and stretching before the bent shape: every such bend whose parts are strips is torn, then a tear is mended again while every piece stays within Stretch Limit without it, so the strip is torn into as few pieces as that takes. When even tearing every bend leaves a piece beyond Stretch Limit, the strip is not torn and stretches as before. Whether the parts fit is first judged by the quick straight-row layouts (no balanced solve), and the tears chosen are checked once by the real layout; the decision is kept, so a Balance step does not try again. Each part keeps its tears as its ends, and each is its own strip. Under Straight Sides None nothing is torn this way.
- <kbd>LMB</kbd> on such a tear takes it back: the strip stays whole there and stretches instead; the taken-back tear is drawn as a skipped tear, and <kbd>LMB</kbd> on it restores it. Like the other skipped tears, these last for the redo panel.

## Straightening lines
Press <kbd>V</kbd> to pick lines of edges to lay straight in UV. <kbd>LMB</kbd> takes the loop through the edge under the cursor: a line running along the strip (a long side, or a line between them on a wide strip) is laid on one horizontal line, a line across the strip (an end, or a line across a wide strip) on one vertical line. A long side or an end is taken whole however it bends; a line inside the strip is followed through quads, triangles and n-gons alike, at every vertex on the edge that keeps its direction best (turning by 30° at most, read on the strip with both long sides straight), up to the boundary. A diagonal lies on no line. <kbd>Alt</kbd>+<kbd>LMB</kbd> takes the one edge under the cursor instead, made horizontal or vertical, whichever is nearer to how it runs now. Any number of lines can be picked, one click each; clicking a straightened line again (any of its edges) frees that line alone. A line belongs to the piece it was picked on (the piece holding the face under the cursor, where an edge lies on a tear between two pieces) and is kept straight on that piece only. The piece is laid out again at once (a quick solve first, the full one once you stop, as with Balance).
- It works under every Straight Sides value. A pick only adds a constraint, it never loosens one: the long sides the piece has straight (after Stretch Limit) stay straight, the line is kept on top, and nothing is relaxed further. On a wide band that Both relaxes to one straight long side, a picked middle line is laid straight while that long side stays straight.
- Only a pick that would change something lights up: under Both on a piece laid out with both long sides straight, the lines along it are straight already; a line is judged on the layout as laid out (after Stretch Limit), not by the setting. A line that could not be kept is not offered either: one joining both long sides (or both ends), or one that would squash a real corner of a face flat (an end with corners on it). Should the layout still not keep a clicked line straight (a face would fold), the click is taken back at once, a warning says so (*UV Stripe: this line cannot be laid straight here*) and that line is not offered again: no pick is stored that the layout ignores.
- Under Straight Sides = None the clicked edge is also its piece's align edge (Align becomes Picked Edge, see *Aligning the real shape*) and is dropped again with its line; when the last such edge goes, Align goes back to what it was. A piece kept straight along its lines is laid out balanced, not unfolded, so the Align row does not show for it.
- The straightened lines are drawn in the viewport in the error colour (red with the default theme) while the interactive mode runs, and the HUD row *Straightened Lines* counts them while there are any. They last for the redo panel; a new run starts without them.

## Aligning the real shape
For every piece laid out with no long side straight (Straight Sides = None for every piece or for that piece, or one Stretch Limit relaxed that far), **Align** (<kbd>G</kbd>) sets what the unfolded piece runs along; the HUD shows its row only then. The piece is still turned only, never mirrored or stretched, with its near row at the bottom.
- *Auto* (default) — the straighter row: its one edge longer than half the row, else the line from its first to its last vertex.
- *Longest Edge* / *Shortest Edge* — the longest or shortest edge of the piece's near and far rows (cross edges never count); on a tie the first one, near row first.
- *Picked Edge* — the edge picked on that piece. Under Straight Sides = None, a click while picking lines to straighten (<kbd>V</kbd>, see *Straightening lines*) also stores the clicked edge, sets Align to Picked Edge and lays the strip out again. An edge of the near or far row runs along the strip; any other edge (a cross edge, an end, a diagonal) stands across it. Each piece keeps one picked edge: a new click on the same piece replaces it, and clicking the stored edge again removes it with its line. An edge where the strip is torn belongs to both pieces beside it. A piece with no picked edge uses Auto. The picked edges in use are drawn in the viewport, green with the default theme. Picking stays on after each click; <kbd>V</kbd>, <kbd>Esc</kbd> or <kbd>RMB</kbd> stop it.

Each piece of a torn strip aligns on its own. Align is kept as a last-used value like the other options, and only UV Stripe uses it: Inset and Edge Slide always align an unfolded piece by Auto. Like the cuts, the picked edges last for the redo panel, and a new run starts without them.

## What counts as a strip
Each edge-connected group of selected faces is one strip:
- **Open chain** — faces in a row, one face wide; it may bend. Quad, triangle or n-gon ends (a quad whose rails or end carry extra vertices, or whose rail steps in or out): an n-gon end is closed where its two rails come out most equal in length, so a step stays in its rail. A single n-gon selected on its own is a strip too.
- **Closed ring** — a loop of faces, such as a band round a cylinder; it is cut open at one rung, or at the cross edge you pick (see *Breaking a ring*). A closed band several faces wide is cut open along a line across it (see *A closed band several faces wide*).
- **Wide patch** — a patch several faces wide (a profile swept along an arc: a ledge, a wall, a flange) with four corners; the two longer sides become the rails, the other two the ends. Quads, triangles and n-gons may mix. The corners are the four boundary vertices used by one face only; when a collapsed vertex took one or two of them (two or three left), the missing ones are the boundary vertices on two faces with the smallest angle within the surface (the sum of their faces' corners there), each sharper than 150° and sharper by more than 15° than every other boundary vertex. A disc, a cap or a patch with no clear corner is skipped. Faces round an inner vertex that each share an edge with two others (a 2 x 2 patch) are read as a patch too, not as a ring. Measured within the surface, a vertex where the patch folds along its end is no corner, and a corner dulled by a collapse still is. When one face uses more than four boundary vertices (an end split into n-gons, or a rounded, fanned end), the vertices nearly in line (turning under 15°) are no corners, and the others are read as two runs, one per end, each end from the first to the last vertex of its run: an end turns by half a turn (give or take 45°) and is shorter than either long side. The reading must stand out by 15° over any other; if it does not, with a seed face the reading that lines up with the seed face's longer edges wins, else the island waits for help (see *Islands that are no strip*). A patch that branches (a T, a plus) is not read as one strip by this rule.

A group that branches or forks is cut into strips (see *Islands that are no strip*). A group that touches itself at a vertex, has a hole (other than a closed band), is a disc or a cap, has faces wound against each other, or is a single triangle is skipped with a warning; the other groups are still unwrapped.

## Islands that are no strip
An island that branches or forks (a T, a plus, a slit) is not one strip. UV Stripe reads it in one of two ways.

**Cut automatically.** The island is cut at its junctions and slits (a cut runs along a line of edges across the strip, from a concave boundary vertex): the fewest parts win, then the part holding the seed face is the largest, and so on. Once cut, every part is its own strip: laid out on its own (Straight Sides, Ends, Stretch Limit, its own tears) and placed like any other strip, so under Fit the parts are stacked at one texel density, the main run first (the run holding the seed face, else the most guide faces, else the most faces), the arms after it; In Place and Active place each part by their own rules. The HUD and the redo report say what was done, and the strip count counts every part.
- <kbd>LMB</kbd> on an automatic junction cut takes it back: that line is not cut again, and the island is cut along another line if one works, else it waits for help. The taken-back cut is drawn as a skipped tear; <kbd>LMB</kbd> on it restores it.

**Help.** When no cut works but picked corners or a cut could help, the island is left as it was. The interactive mode still starts (the other islands are laid out meanwhile), draws the island's boundary in the warning colour with its corner candidates, and a HUD note asks *Click four corners, or click an edge to cut the island into strips*.
- <kbd>LMB</kbd> near a boundary vertex picks or unpicks a corner; four corners make the island one strip with those corners.
- <kbd>LMB</kbd> on an inner edge cuts the island along the line of edges through it (<kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>LMB</kbd>: that edge alone); clicking the edge again removes that cut, and the cuts add up. Cuts by hand on a strip several faces wide work the same way, and each part is its own strip.
- The redo panel and a script call lay out what they can and warn which islands need the interactive mode.
- A disc, a cap, a patch with a hole, or faces wound against each other are still skipped: an island waits for help only when a cut or picked corners could fix it, that is when its boundary has a concave vertex (a branch, a fork, a slit) or exactly four clear groups of corners (a patch whose corners only tie, such as a square with chamfered corners). A ring of quads round an n-gon, all selected, is a disc and is skipped.
- A cut part is always laid out along the strip it was cut from: a part that would read across the strip (a few columns cut off near an end) takes its cuts as its ends. A picked edge with no line of edges across the island through it cuts nothing, and the HUD says so.
- Picked corners and cuts last for the redo panel; a new run starts without them.

## Seed and guide faces
Two kinds of face help UV Stripe read an island and decide where it starts. Neither changes an island that already reads as one strip.
- <kbd>Alt</kbd>+<kbd>LMB</kbd> on a face makes it its island's seed face (one per island; click again to remove it). The strip starts there: U grows away from the seed face (a seed touching one end puts that end at U = 0, otherwise U runs along the seed's longer pair of edges), and at a branch its run is the main one. With no seed picked, the mesh's active face does this for islands that need resolving (a branch or unclear corners).
- <kbd>Shift</kbd>+<kbd>Alt</kbd>+<kbd>LMB</kbd> adds or removes a guide face: at a branch, the run with the most guide faces is the main one.

They are drawn in the viewport (the seed's outline and centre, the guides' outlines and centres), the HUD row *Seed and Guide Faces* appears while any is set, and they last for the redo panel. A plain click on a face still does nothing.

## Direction
The result does not depend on how the mesh was built: an open strip runs from its end with the smaller coordinate on its main world axis (U = 0) toward the other end. A ring's lower boundary along its main axis lies on V = 0; for a flat ring it is the outer boundary.

## Options
All options are also in the redo panel. They are the settings of every UV Stripe layout in iOps: Inset and Edge Slide lay their strips out with the values UV Stripe was last used with.
- **Mode** — *Active* (default) lays out the strip holding the active face as In Place does, then fits every other piece to its UV bounding box without stretching it: each piece is scaled by one factor for both axes (see **Fit to Active**) and centred on the reference. A piece is never turned: every piece of a run is laid out by the same Orientation, so its length already runs along the reference's. With no active face among the selected faces, or no UV map to place it by, everything is laid out In Place and a short note says so. *Fit 0–1* scales all strips by one factor so the longest spans U 0–1, keeping one texel density, and stacks them upward. *In Place* keeps each strip where its old UVs were, at the texel density of the neighbouring faces. *Stack* scales the strips as Fit 0–1 does, then centres every one on the middle of 0–1, so they lie on top of each other instead of stacked upward; Random Offset, when set, shifts them after that.
- **Fit to Active** — used by Active; every choice scales a piece by one factor, so it keeps its proportions. *Height* (default) matches the reference's height, across the strip, the width following by the same factor; *Width* matches its width, along the strip; both follow Orientation (under Vertical the height across the strip is the U extent); *Width and Height* fits the piece inside the reference's bounding box. For trim textures: Height puts every piece on the reference's band at one texel density, running past it along U; with Orientation = Vertical the band runs along V, so use Width.
- **Orientation** — *Horizontal* (default) runs the strip along U. *Vertical* turns it 90° counter-clockwise, never mirrored: Fit spans V 0–1 and stacks the strips to the right, In Place turns each strip about its own place. Under Active the reference strip turns and the other pieces follow it.
- **Straight Sides** — what is straight along every piece. *Both* (default): both long sides and every line of edges running along the piece straight and horizontal. *Longer Side* / *Shorter Side*: only that long side (by its 3D length; the near one on a tie) straight, the other long side and the lines inside placed by the least stretch alone. *None*: nothing straightened, every face unfolded at its real shape (each face keeps its shape and stays joined to its neighbours, the piece turned by **Align**, never mirrored); a piece that cannot unfold flat keeps its straighter row straight and places every other vertex at its real distance from it.
- **Ends** — which ends stand upright (across the strip, at a right angle to its length). *Upright Where Cheap* (default, since 2026-11-24): on a strip several faces wide both ends stand upright while that folds no face and stretches none beyond Stretch Limit, however much less free ends would stretch; only when upright ends fold a face or stretch one beyond the limit are free ends taken (when they stay within it; with neither within the limit, the free ends only when they stretch the worst face more than 0.05 less). A strip one face wide keeps its real slant, as under Slanted. *Slanted*: both free, the real slant: the long sides keep their true lengths, and a quad arc comes out as a trapezoid outline. No layout that stands the ends upright competes under Slanted. *Both*: both upright, whatever it costs (when they fold a face, each end alone is tried, then free ends). An upright end stands only its own line: the longest run of the end with no corner sharper than 150° within the surface, so a chamfer or a rounded corner beside it keeps its own slant (an end with no such corner stands whole). *Longer End* / *Shorter End*: only the end whose cross edges are longer / shorter in 3D (on a tie the end at U = 0). On a strip one face wide an upright end is its cross edge standing vertical. Not used under Straight Sides = None.
- **How a piece is laid out** — UV Stripe measures every layout that keeps what is asked and takes the one whose worst face stretches least (the first on a tie); a layout that folds a face loses to one that does not. Both long sides with free ends (Slanted): the rows at their true lengths with the ends at their real slant (the faces of a flat piece between straight rails at their exact shape), or the balanced layout with free ends. Both long sides with both ends upright (Both): the layouts with upright ends: every cross edge vertical (each quad a rectangle), each row by its own length scaled to the mean (Even Rows), or balanced with upright ends. One end upright (Longer End, Shorter End): balanced. A straight long side holds its own line: a notch in it (a short run that turns sharply one way and back onto the line, at most 6 edges) keeps its depth. One long side straight: balanced, with the ends as asked; the other long side relaxes, and the result is never worse than with both long sides straight (that layout stands in when it stretches less). The balanced layouts, the costly ones, are only tried on a strip one face wide when none of the straight-row layouts already lays it out with no face stretched beyond 5 %. The balanced layout spreads the stretch that cannot be avoided over all the faces, quads, triangles and n-gons alike, instead of letting it land on one row, and keeps straight exactly what is asked. When every layout that keeps what is asked folds a face, a straight-row layout that folds fewer is taken instead.
- **Tears** — a strip one face wide is torn where its bends make the faces stretch: every bend is a candidate; a candidate that cannot be torn yet is tried again once the others are in (so a rectangular frame tears at all four corners into equal pieces, never three), and a candidate is dropped when the faces stretch no more without it, or stretch no more than Stretch Limit, so the strip is torn into as few pieces as keep every face within the limit: a sharp corner is torn, a gentle arc is not, and a strip that only folds is never torn. A strip that one of the layouts it will be laid out with (balanced at the current Balance among them) lays out whole within Stretch Limit and with no fold is not torn at all. Under Ends = Slanted (and Upright Where Cheap, which keeps a strip one face wide slanted) the tears are measured with the slant-keeping layout too. A patch several faces wide is never torn. Seams (Split at Seams), cuts marked by hand, skipped tears and a ring's break apply as described above. A ring laid out whole keeps every row at the mean length, closed.
- **Stretch Limit** — 1.25 by default, 1.0 to 3.0 (<kbd>Alt</kbd>+<kbd>Wheel</kbd> stops at 1.05; at 1.0 every piece would relax to its real shape): a piece whose worst face stretches more than this is relaxed one step at a time, both long sides → the longer one only → none (its real shape), and stops at the first step within the limit, else at the least stretched. On a strip several faces wide the limit alone decides: the strictest shape within it stands (both long sides straight, so the corners stay square, and under Upright Where Cheap the ends upright), however much less a looser one would stretch; a looser shape is taken only when every stricter one stretches beyond the limit. When no shape is within the limit, a stricter one stands unless the looser one stretches the worst face more than 0.05 less. A piece's own Straight Sides (see *Selecting pieces*) is relaxed the same way. At 1.0 every piece that stretches at all is relaxed. Its HUD row (*Stretch Limit (Alt + Wheel)*) shows only while it applies, that is, when a piece was relaxed by it.
- **Balance** — a slider from 0 to 1, 0.5 by default (<kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Wheel</kbd> ±0.1, <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Shift</kbd>+<kbd>Wheel</kbd> ±0.02, <kbd>B</kbd> jumps 0 → 0.5 → 1 → 0). At 0 the layout has the least stretch, with the texel density differing between faces; at 1 every face stays near its real size: an even texel density, the rigid layout, with some stretch left where the rows must differ in length. In between the layout slides straight from one to the other, so the stretch and the texel spread move with the slider: on the test arcs the stretch falls and the texel spread rises steadily from 1 to 0, and 0.5 lies strictly between. It applies to every balanced piece, one face wide too. It never folds a face; where the least-stretch end cannot be reached, the rigid layout (1) stands. Saved values from older versions, *Even Texel* and *Least Stretch*, read as 1 and 0.
- **Split at Seams** — on by default: seams already inside a strip tear it into pieces; a ring with a single seam is cut open there instead of at its sharpest corner. Off, the strip is laid out across them (and Mark Seams clears them).
- **Mark Seams** — makes the seams match the pieces: every piece's outline (tears, and where a ring is cut open) is marked, and the seams inside a piece are cleared. Seams outside the selection are never touched; with Mark Seams off no seam changes.
- **Random Offset** — *Along Strip* (default) shifts every piece but the Active reference by a random amount along the strip's length (U for a horizontal strip, V for a vertical one), so strips stacked on one reference show different parts of the texture. *Across Strip* shifts across the strip, *Both* along and across, *None* leaves the pieces where the mode put them.
- **Offset Limit** — the largest random shift, in UV units (0.25 by default): each piece moves by a random amount between minus and plus this.
- **Offset Seed** — the same seed gives the same arrangement every time; change it for another.
- **Merge Doubles** — on by default (it changes the mesh only where it finds doubles). Vertices of the strip closer than 1 % of its typical edge (an unmerged double, such as two rail vertices 0.0002 apart) are always treated as one point, so they never skew the layout, and with Merge Doubles off UV Stripe warns how many it found. On, it merges exactly those vertices before laying the strip out (in the interactive mode, on confirm; the preview already looks the same).

## Stretched faces
After the layout every laid-out face is measured against its real shape: how much more its UVs scale it along one direction than along another (a uniform scale or a turn does not count). Every face is measured on the triangles a renderer could split it into: a fan round its centre (the mean of its corners) and each of its real corners (150° or sharper); its stretch is the larger of the fan's average and its worst single triangle, so a face measures the same whichever corner comes first, a corner or an edge squashed flat in UV counts as fully stretched however healthy the rest of the face looks, and a strongly twisted (non-planar) quad shows its twist as stretch. The least-stretch layout minimises every one of those triangles, so it cannot hide stretch in one, and a layout that squashes a real corner flat counts as folded. With this honest measure a trapezoid laid as a rectangle shows the shear of its corner triangles: a one-face-wide arc kept straight typically measures 1.15–1.3. A face stretched by more than 5 % is drawn in the viewport, the HUD counts them, and the redo panel or a script reports the count as a warning. Typical causes: Ends = Both on a strip that bends, a skipped tear, a long side clicked straight on a piece that tapers, and rails that curve.

## Tips
- With the defaults (Straight Sides Both, Ends Upright Where Cheap, Balance 0.5, Stretch Limit 1.25) a flat strip bending in its own plane comes out as stretch-free pieces, torn only where a tear lowers the stretch; a tapered piece that both straight sides would stretch beyond the limit keeps only its longer side straight, or its real shape. A strip whose rails curve, or one that both bends and twists, is laid out as close as straight sides allow.
- For a trim sheet, keep Straight Sides on Both and set Ends to Both where the ends must meet the trim's edges; select a piece and press <kbd>Q</kbd> or <kbd>W</kbd> where one piece needs something else.
- For an island that branches, <kbd>Alt</kbd>+<kbd>LMB</kbd> the face where the strip should start, and <kbd>Shift</kbd>+<kbd>Alt</kbd>+<kbd>LMB</kbd> faces of the run that should stay the main one.
- Pinned UVs and the selection are left as they are.
- A mesh without a UV map gets one; cancelling the interactive mode removes it again.
